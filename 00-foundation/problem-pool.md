# Problems that teams found

Each team member conducted research, observed the people around them, and identified the following problems:

```
AUTHOR: Archil Sanikidze
WHO I watched: My mother, an individual entrepreneur operating under the 1% Small Business Status in Georgia.
STRUGGLE I saw: Every month before the 15th deadline, she logs into the cluttered rs.ge portal, gets confused by the declaration terminology and tax input fields, and spends over an hour in distress trying to figure out what amount to submit before giving up and calling an accountant.
COST I can name: 50–100 GEL per month paid to a private accountant for a 2-minute declaration task
```

```
AUTHOR: Archil Sanikidze
WHO I watched: My friend Luka, a university student with strong mathematics skills trying to launch an independent math tutoring practice.
STRUGGLE I saw: Every evening, he spends hours manually scrolling through unstructured Facebook groups, typing repetitive "I can teach" comments on parent requests, and constantly re-posting his announcement as it gets buried in group feeds within minutes.
COST I can name: Hours of daily wasted time doing manual social media outreach, remaining hidden from parents outside his neighborhood, and lost potential tutoring income.
```

```
AUTHOR: Ani Purtskhvanidze
WHO I watched: my friend Giorgi, driving to a city center appointment
STRUGGLE I saw: he circled the same 3 streets looking for a free spot, with no way to know where one was
COST I can name: 20 minutes of driving around, arrived 15 minutes late to the appointment
```

```
AUTHOR: Ani Purtskhvanidze
WHO I watched: my friend Nika, driving to an office appointment in the city center at 16:00
STRUGGLE I saw: he circled the same 3 streets looking for a free spot, then parked far away and walked, because there was no way to know where a space was free
COST I can name: 20 minutes driving around, arrived 15 minutes late to the appointment
```

```
AUTHOR: Ani Purtskhvanidze
WHO I watched: my friend Levani, a student at KIU taking 5 courses
STRUGGLE I saw: he kept deadlines in different chats and notes, missed an assignment date, and only found out when the class discussed it
COST I can name: 1 assignment submitted late and 10 points lost
```



```
AUTHOR: Saba tabukashvili
WHO I watched: my friend Giorgi, a student looking for a job in Kutaisi
STRUGGLE I saw: he searched job sites for 3 months and found that most listings ask for 3+ years of experience, 3 languages and 10 hours of work a day, and offer only 1200 GEL
COST I can name: 98 applications sent, 30 replies (27 rejections, 3 interviews), 0 offers, and 13 weeks without a job
```



```
AUTHOR: Saba Tabukashvili
WHO I watched: my friend Nino, a student from another city who moved to Kutaisi and doesn't know anyone here
STRUGGLE I saw: she searched through Facebook groups and listing sites, but many ads were outdated, had no real photos or a clear price, and she had to call each owner to find out if the place was still available. She wanted to share an apartment with a roommate to save money, but had no way to find a trustworthy one, so she had to rent alone
COST I can name: about 10 phone calls, 6-7 apartment visits, a few months of searching, and she ended up paying 900 GEL per month more than she would have with a roommate

```

```
AUTHOR: Saba Tabukashvili
WHO I watched: my friend Karli, a student at Free University who wants to tutor school students in physics and math alongside his studies
STRUGGLE I saw: as a beginner tutor nobody knew him, so he joined a company that finds students in exchange for a share of his income. He got only 3 students (1 in physics, 2 in math), and each needed different materials, so he prepared separately for every lesson. On top of that, he struggled to build a schedule that worked for all three students at once
COST I can name: hours of preparation for just 3 students, a large share of his income going to the company, so he earned little for the time spent. In the end he quit the company and now has to find students on his own
```

```
AUTHOR: Mate Gvetadze
WHO I watched: Tamar, the owner of a small neighborhood grocery shop in Kutaisi where regular customers buy "on credit" and pay at the end of the month
STRUGGLE I saw: she writes every credit purchase by hand in a paper notebook ("nisia"). The pages are messy, some customers say they already paid or that the amount is wrong, and she has no easy way to see the total a person owes or to remind them. When a customer asks "how much do I owe?", she spends several minutes flipping pages and adding numbers with a calculator while other customers wait in line
COST I can name: around 40 customers in the notebook, about 30 minutes every evening adding up totals, roughly 300-400 GEL per month that is never paid back because nobody can prove the amount, and regular arguments with customers
```

```
AUTHOR: Mate Gvetadze
WHO I watched: my friend Irakli, a barber who runs a small 2-chair barbershop in Kutaisi and takes bookings only through phone calls and Instagram messages
STRUGGLE I saw: while cutting hair he has to stop to answer calls and messages, and he keeps appointments in his head or on a piece of paper. Twice in one week he booked two clients for the same time, and clients who forget their appointment simply don't show up, leaving him with an empty chair. Clients also complain they can't see which times are free without calling
COST I can name: 10-15 interruptions per day to answer the phone, about 5 no-shows per week (around 100 GEL lost weekly at 20 GEL per haircut), and 2 angry clients who left after a double booking
```

```
AUTHOR: Mate Gvetadze
WHO I watched: my friend Luka, a KIU student who travels to the university every day by marshrutka (minibus) from the other side of Kutaisi
STRUGGLE I saw: there is no real schedule or live information about when the next marshrutka will arrive or which route passes his stop. He waits at the stop not knowing if the bus is 2 minutes or 25 minutes away, and when a route changes or a marshrutka is full he only finds out standing there. Many times he gives up and takes a taxi so he won't be late for his lecture
COST I can name: on average 15-20 minutes of waiting per trip (about 3 hours per week), 2-3 taxi rides per week at 5-7 GEL each (around 60 GEL per month), and being late to the first lecture at least once a week
```

```
AUTHOR: Saba
WHO I watched: Low-income families in my neighborhood who rely on informal hand-me-downs and donated items from local neighbors and relatives.
STRUGGLE I saw: Whenever they urgently need seasonal clothing, children's supplies, or basic household items, they are restricted to asking a small circle of immediate neighbors, facing emotional discomfort and often coming up empty-handed due to their limited local network.
COST I can name: Days spent without essential household items, emotional strain and awkwardness from repeated personal solicitation, and complete reliance on immediate geographical surroundings.
```
