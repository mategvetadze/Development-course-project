The quote I heard myself that mattered most, with its log number:

"I have to open many job posts to find out whether I'm actually eligible." (01)

One moment I broke the Mom Test, and what I asked instead next time (name the log):

my interview questions were --> 
1. What are you studying, and what year are you in?
2. Are you currently working somewhere, or are you doing an internship?
3. Where do you usually look for job openings or internships?
4. When was the last time you checked these platforms? And before that?
5. What device do you mainly use when looking for opportunities — your phone or your laptop?
6. How do you decide whether a particular job opening is relevant to you?
7. Think about the last time you sat down to look for a job. When exactly was that?
8. What was the most difficult, tiring, or frustrating part of that particular job-search process?
9. How did that search process end? Did you apply for any positions or send out your CV?
10. Over the past year, how many times has it happened that you found out about an interesting job opportunity too late or missed the application deadline?
11. Approximately how many applications have you submitted in the past three months?
12. Have you ever tried using any tools or platforms to make your job search easier?

 In log 09 I asked question 12: "Have you ever tried using any tools or platforms to make your job search easier?"
 This invites the person to talk about solutions instead of what they actually did, and the answer was a hypothetical:
 "I don't know if such tools exist, it would be good if they do." Next time I would ask about a real past event instead: 
 "Tell me about the last time a job post took you a long time to check. What did you do next?"

## 2. Pattern journal, Weeks 1 to 4



**Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role**

The first week was probably one of the most important parts of this course, and for me too, because I had to find people (classmates) to form a group with, 
choose a problem, work with them on this problem and project, make various decisions together with them, and work throughout the whole semester. 
Fortunately, forming the team was completed successfully, because I found 3 such people and we created a team. 
In the first week I created the team contract (I founded this team), we of course talked to each other, divided the responsibilities by agreement, 
and came to one opinion. Our main decision is that nobody does only what they are supposed to, nobody works alone on only their own share of the work, 
and every member of the team does everything in agreement with the team. If someone runs into a problem, they do not try to handle it alone.
We also created our group on social media, and if anyone needs help, we share our problems with each other and the whole team works on solving them. 
That is exactly why I said the first week's assignment was very important: for developing a product and working on the project correctly, 
the first thing needed is to create the right team, and I believe we did this successfully, and it is not only my merit but also the merit of the other members.

[link: 00-foundation/team-contract.md](https://github.com/mategvetadze/Development-course-project/blob/main/00-foundation/team-contract.md)


**Week 2 · Evidence over opinion**



Our main work in week 2 was that each team member brought 2-3 problems, we reviewed them together,
and after going through a process we chose one to work on. Like the others, 
I thought about which problems are relevant for students and people today,
and what problems I hear about from people every day. Two came to mind.

1. Student housing. Most applicants in Georgia move from the regions to Tbilisi to study,
 and where to live is one of their biggest worries. Some stay with relatives, even though nobody wants that,
 because they want to live independently. Most rent an apartment as a group to split the costs.
 The problem is that many people cannot afford to rent, since it is very expensive (sometimes more than a person's salary),
 and many universities have no campuses the way KIU does. Many go alone without a friend to share with, and it is hard to find an apartment
   that is close to the university, in decent condition, and affordable.

 2. Looking for a job. This problem also came from students, but I think it affects the whole country.
 It is very hard to find a job that fits your schedule, helps you develop, has a good environment, and pays fairly.
 Students often complain that finding one takes months or even years. A simple example is the question "why do most students end up living abroad?"
The main reason is jobs: people are valued more abroad and find a good job more easily than here.

I wrote my two problems (student housing and job search) in the problem pool and took part in choosing the final one.
[link: 00-foundation/problem-pool.md]

**Week 3 · Kill your favorite idea**

In week 3 the team members reviewed each problem, and in the end we chose the job-search problem as our working topic. 
I was glad because it was the problem I had proposed, and everyone agreed that it is a real and current problem. 
After that we wrote the interview questions together and planned who would interview whom and when. 
I chose people I am in contact with, who had told me in the past that they struggled to find a job. 
Even though I knew their situation beforehand, the logs still surprised me: 
in 01 she skipped jobs she liked because she could not tell if she qualified, and in 02 he was looking for an internship abroad,
not the part-time job we had assumed. Our prediction ("students do not raise too few jobs or unfair pay on their own")
was killed by the logs (see DECISIONS.md).

My personal work this week was to conduct interviews 01, 02 and 09 with students, 
present them to the team, and discuss and analyse them together.
I then personally created and uploaded the files: DECISIONS.md, four-filters-scorecard.md,
