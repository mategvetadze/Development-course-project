# Pattern Journal · Mate Gvetadze (@mategvetadze)

I am writing these week notes late (18 October 2026), looking back at what we actually put in the repo. I did not write them in the same week they happened.

## Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role

My main job on the contract is Build — the GitHub repo, fullstack code later, shared with Ani. On September 28 I made the repo for the team and we all signed the contract.

There is one rule in that contract I kept thinking about: you are not allowed to say "that is not my role" when someone asks about the product. So even though my lane is code, I still had to look for real problems. I went and watched people I actually know.

Tamar runs a small shop and writes "nisia" (credit) in a paper notebook. Customers argue about how much they owe, and she spends forever adding numbers while people wait in line. My friend Irakli is a barber — phone rings while he cuts hair, he double-booked twice in one week, no-shows left the chair empty. My friend Luka takes marshrutka to KIU every day and has no idea when the next one comes, so he often pays for a taxi so he is not late.

I wrote those three into the problem pool with struggle + cost, not "here is my app idea." That felt weird at first because my brain jumped to solutions, but the assignment wanted the problem, not the product.

## Week 2 · Evidence over opinion

We put problems on the four-filters scorecard. My nisia / grocery credit thing became Problem C. It scored 7/8 — we can reach Tamar, Tea and Mari, and we could test a Google Sheet next to her notebook for a week. Job search only scored 5. Still, the team picked job search, mostly because of Saba's friend Giorgi: 98 applications, 0 offers, 13 weeks. That number was hard to argue with. Tutor finding students was the runner-up.

Honestly I liked my problem and I thought 7 was a strong score, but I also get why we went for job search — we are all students, we can interview people this week, and we did not know yet if the pain was "too few jobs" or something else. I did not run the Google Sheet test with Tamar that week. We moved on.

## Week 3 · Kill your favorite idea

I ran and logged interviews 10 and 11 (first-year and fourth-year CS at KIU; teammates already had 01–09). Both hit inflated junior posts and scattered channels. The first-year raised few openings at his level + unfair internship pay/hours, then saved two posts and never applied. The fourth-year applies with a tracker (8–10 apps, some replies) and mostly complains about fake junior labels, fragmented LinkedIn/Telegram/local boards, and Capstone time — not “too few jobs” overall. So my two logs disagree a bit on the old prediction, but they agree on posting quality + fragmentation.

Links: [Interview 10](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/10-student-cs.md), [Interview 11](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/11-student-cs.md), [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md)

## Week 4 · The prototype is the conversation

From the patterns we wrote a problem statement: students lose time because posts are scattered and mixed with junk. Then we made a prototype plan that is still paper — five job cards, student says apply or skip without opening the full post. If too many people open the envelope, our assumption is wrong and we should not start coding a fancy card UI yet. My interviews feed that: 10 cannot tell what he is allowed to apply for; 11 reads the stack and still fights fake junior posts.

On the tester list I am MG for T4 (someone new, not interviewed before). That is only a plan so far. I have not messaged that person yet, I have not run a session, and I have not cut the paper cards. So Week 4 for me is still "I understand what we will test," not "I already tested it."
