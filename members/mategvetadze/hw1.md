"The hardest part is that almost everything says 'junior' but wants experience, and I can't tell what I'm actually allowed to apply for." (10)

This stuck with me because it is the same "am I eligible?" pain as in log 01, but from a first-year who barely applies at all. Interview 11 made the contrast sharper: the 4th-year uses a tracker, Notion, and filtered LinkedIn alerts, submits 8–10 applications, and still names inflated junior requirements and fragmented channels. So the problem is not only “lazy searching” — even an organized senior hits the same posting mess. Openings spread across many places also matches our Week 4 problem statement.

One moment I broke the Mom Test, and what I would ask instead next time (Interview 10):

Near the end I asked the script question: "Have you ever tried using any tools or platforms to make your job search easier?" That kind of question pushes toward solutions / wishes. He still answered with a real story (LinkedIn alerts full of senior roles, so he ignores them now), which was lucky. In interview 11 the same question got a cleaner tools list (alerts + Notion), which is useful, but next time I would still ask behavior first: "Tell me about the last job alert email you got. What did it say, and what did you do with it?"

## 2. Pattern journal, Weeks 1 to 4

Same text as in [my pattern journal](pattern-journal.md). Written late on 18 October 2026 from the repo + what I remember. Not written week by week in real time.

**Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role**

My main job on the contract is Build — the GitHub repo, fullstack code later, shared with Ani. On September 28 I made the repo for the team and we all signed the contract.

There is one rule in that contract I kept thinking about: you are not allowed to say "that is not my role" when someone asks about the product. So even though my lane is code, I still had to look for real problems. I went and watched people I actually know.

Tamar runs a small shop and writes "nisia" (credit) in a paper notebook. Customers argue about how much they owe, and she spends forever adding numbers while people wait in line. My friend Irakli is a barber — phone rings while he cuts hair, he double-booked twice in one week, no-shows left the chair empty. My friend Luka takes marshrutka to KIU every day and has no idea when the next one comes, so he often pays for a taxi so he is not late.

I wrote those three into the problem pool with struggle + cost, not "here is my app idea." That felt weird at first because my brain jumped to solutions, but the assignment wanted the problem, not the product.

**Week 2 · Evidence over opinion**

We put problems on the four-filters scorecard. My nisia / grocery credit thing became Problem C. It scored 7/8 — we can reach Tamar, Tea and Mari, and we could test a Google Sheet next to her notebook for a week. Job search only scored 5. Still, the team picked job search, mostly because of Saba's friend Giorgi: 98 applications, 0 offers, 13 weeks. That number was hard to argue with. Tutor finding students was the runner-up.

Honestly I liked my problem and I thought 7 was a strong score, but I also get why we went for job search — we are all students, we can interview people this week, and we did not know yet if the pain was "too few jobs" or something else. I did not run the Google Sheet test with Tamar that week. We moved on.

[problem pool](../../00-foundation/problem-pool.md) · [four-filters](../../00-foundation/four-filters-scorecard.md) · [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md)

**Week 3 · Kill your favorite idea**

I ran and logged interviews 10 and 11 (first-year and fourth-year CS at KIU). Teammates already had 01–09. Both of my students hit inflated “junior” posts and scattered channels. The first-year raised few openings at his level and unfair internship pay/hours, then saved two posts and never applied. The fourth-year did not really say “too few jobs” or “pay is unfair” — he said junior labels lie, channels are fragmented, and Capstone eats his time — and he still submitted 8–10 applications with some replies. So my two logs pull in different directions on the old prediction, but they agree on posting quality + fragmentation.

[Interview 10](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/10-student-cs.md) · [Interview 11](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/11-student-cs.md) · [DECISIONS.md](../../DECISIONS.md)

**Week 4 · The prototype is the conversation**

From the patterns we wrote a problem statement: students lose time because posts are scattered and mixed with junk. Then we made a prototype plan that is still paper — five job cards, student says apply or skip without opening the full post. If too many people open the envelope, our assumption is wrong and we should not start coding a fancy card UI yet. My interviews feed that test: 10 cannot tell what he is “allowed” to apply for; 11 reads the stack carefully and still fights fake junior posts. A card with requirements / hours / salary / deadline is exactly what both of them are missing when they skim on the phone.

On the tester list I am MG for T4 (someone new, not interviewed before). That is only a plan so far. I have not messaged that person yet, I have not run a session, and I have not cut the paper cards. So Week 4 for me is still "I understand what we will test," not "I already tested it."
