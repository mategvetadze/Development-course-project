## The script you will read from

**Opening (read aloud):** “We are studying how students find opportunities related to their careers. Please describe what actually happened; there are no right answers. May I take anonymous notes for our student project? May I record this conversation? Recording is optional, and we can continue with notes only.” Record the answers before proceeding; do not include names in shared notes. Ask the main questions in order, and use only follow-ups that fit the answer.

1. Think about the last time you looked for a job, internship, or another opportunity connected to your career. When was it? What were you studying then, and were you working or interning at the time? **If no search comes to mind:** Ask about the last specific opportunity they considered.
2. Walk me through that search from what prompted you to start until you stopped. **If needed:** What did you open first? Where did you go next, and why? What did you actually use to save, filter, or compare what you found? If they describe a method, ask for the last time it helped or failed; if their search went smoothly, ask what worked.
3. Choose one result you spent time on in that search. What happened from opening it to deciding what to do? **If needed:** What made it seem relevant or irrelevant? What, if anything, did you have to check elsewhere? Which steps, if any, took extra effort? Did that change your decision, or only take extra time? If eligibility came up: What did you learn when you checked whether you could apply?
4. If a different opportunity comes to mind that you considered but did not apply to, or put off, tell me what happened. **If needed:** What did you know at the time? What did you do to resolve any uncertainty? What finally stopped or delayed you? If they mention studies or timing, ask how those affected that particular decision. If none, move on.
5. Can you recall a career opportunity you learned about only after you could no longer take part? If so, tell me about the most recent one. **If needed:** What was it? Where and when did you discover it? Do you know where and when it was first announced? Which sources had you checked before you found out? Do you know why it had not appeared in your search? Could you have applied or attended while it was open, and what makes you think so? What did you do afterward? If they describe a way they now avoid such misses, ask when it last worked. If none comes to mind, ask what has helped them stay aware of opportunities and move on.
6. In a past search, what else, if anything, did you look for to help you enter your field besides a job or internship? Tell me about the most recent example. **If needed:** What led you to it: your own search or something you happened to see? Why did you consider it relevant? Did you apply or attend? What happened afterward? If they mention a course, training program, hackathon, or event they did not join, ask what happened between finding it and deciding not to join. If none, accept that answer.
7. Who else should I talk to about this?

For a concrete story, use short follow-ups such as “What happened next?”, “Can you give me an example?”, and “How did you find that out?” Use only the probes relevant to the answer; do not ask for dates, time spent, or causes the participant cannot recall. Note whether each difficulty was volunteered before a topic was introduced by the interviewer.

Self-check: The research questions refer to actual past searches or opportunities. The script asks about actions and outcomes, accepts “none,” and does not ask for opinions about a proposed product.
