## The script you will read from

**Opening (read aloud):** “We are studying how students find opportunities related to their careers. Please describe what actually happened; there are no right answers. May I take anonymous notes for our student project? May I record this conversation? Recording is optional, and we can continue with notes only.” Record the answers before proceeding; do not include names in shared notes. Ask the main questions in order, and use only follow-ups that fit the answer.

1. Think about the last time you looked for a job, internship, or another opportunity connected to your career. When was it? What were you studying then, and were you working or interning at the time?
2. Walk me through that search from what prompted you to start until you stopped. **If needed:** What did you open first? Where did you go next? What made you switch sources? How did you keep track of what you found?
3. Tell me about one opportunity from that search that caught your attention. How did you decide what to do with it? **If needed:** What did the posting say? What information did you check elsewhere? What did you do next, and how did it end? If eligibility came up: What did you learn when you checked whether you could apply?
4. Was there an opportunity you considered but did not apply to, or put off, during that search? If so, tell me what happened. **If needed:** What information did you have at the time? What, if anything, did you do to check eligibility? What led to your decision? If they mention studies or timing, ask how those affected the decision. If the answer is no, move on.
5. Can you recall a career opportunity you learned about only after you could no longer take part? If so, tell me about the most recent one. **If needed:** What was it? Where and when did you first hear of it? Do you know where it had been announced? What happened afterward? Had you been considering applying or attending? If none comes to mind, move on.
6. In a past search, what else, if anything, did you look for to help you enter your field besides a job or internship? Tell me about the most recent example. **If needed:** Was it a course, training program, hackathon, or event? Where did you find it? Did you apply or attend? What happened afterward? If none, accept that answer.
7. Who else should I talk to about this?

For a concrete story, use short follow-ups such as “What happened next?”, “Can you give me an example?”, and “How did you find that out?” Do not ask a follow-up that the participant has already answered. Note whether each difficulty was volunteered before a topic was introduced by the interviewer.

---

Self-check: The research questions refer to actual past searches or opportunities. The script asks about actions and outcomes, accepts “none,” and does not ask for opinions about a proposed product.
