# Problem Statement

## The statement

University students in Georgia who are searching for an internship or an entry-level job have to look, every time they search for an opening, across several websites, company pages, social networks and university channels (01, 03, 04, 05, 06). They open and read post after post, many of them irrelevant or duplicated, to work out whether each one is relevant and whether they qualify (01, 04, 06, 07, 08). This costs them missed deadlines (01 found a vacancy after its deadline, 08 saw an internship the day after its deadline, 07 estimates 2-3 misses) and, in some cases, a course, a legal clinic or an internship they learned about too late (04, 06, 08), on top of 1-2 hours of searching on each weekend day (09). Today they check LinkedIn, jobs.ge, company pages and university channels by hand (04, 05), search Facebook (07), ask friends (05), and sometimes message people to learn the salary and the hours (08). That fails them because, as 01 says, "I have to open many job posts to find out whether I'm actually eligible," and as 08 says, "Too many posts, and half of them are the same job posted again by different people" (01, 08).

## What the evidence ruled out

The problem we believed before interviewing: there are too few jobs in the students' field, or the pay and requirements offered are unfair.

- Not established across the logs. Only 09 raised pay against requirements unprompted (1500 GEL for 6 office days, 3 languages, 5+ years of experience). 06 described a "junior" role asking for 3 years of experience, and 07 was told a "junior" posting needed experience. Most logs describe finding and sorting posts instead (01, 04, 06, 07, 08). Our Week 3 verdict on the original prediction is "killed" (see DECISIONS.md).

## What is still open

- 03, 04 and 06 searched often and still missed opportunities, so irregular checking does not explain every miss (03, 04, 06). Question for the next interview: "The last time you missed something, where and when did you first see it?"

## Pattern behind it

- Pattern: Searching across scattered sources and sorting through irrelevant posts makes relevant opportunities hard to find.
- Root cause: In the reported searches, dispersed sources and mixed or duplicate postings make students check opportunities manually to identify relevant ones (01, 04, 06, 07, 08).
