## Original hypothesis (preserved)

In Georgia as a whole, not just in Kutaisi, there is a clear shortage of job opportunities. Every member of our team agreed that one of the biggest challenges students face is finding a job related to their field of study. Even a quick look at the situation in Tbilisi shows that many students work in hotels, restaurants, as couriers, and in similar positions while studying architecture, engineering, medicine, and other fields.

Another major issue connected to this problem is **the lack of fair compensation and recognition**. For example, we often come across job advertisements from companies looking for an IT programmer who is expected to work six days a week, ten hours a day, speak Georgian, English, and Russian, communicate effectively, work well in a team, be responsible, and possess many other skills. Yet, the salary offered is only 1,000 GEL. This is a clear example of undervaluing employees: companies expect candidates to meet numerous requirements while offering a salary that does not adequately reflect their skills and workload. This is another reason why many students struggle to find work in their chosen fields.

After discussing these issues, our team decided to develop a product that would help not only students but also other job seekers address these challenges, or at least make them less difficult to overcome. Our goal is to help people find suitable job opportunities that support their career development, fit their schedules, offer fair compensation, and ensure that their skills and efforts are valued.
