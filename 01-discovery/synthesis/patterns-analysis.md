# Patterns Analysis

## Pattern 1: Searching across scattered sources and sorting through irrelevant posts makes relevant opportunities hard to find.

Interviews behind it: `01`, `03`, `04`, `05`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09`.

1. Why are relevant opportunities hard to find? Participants describe opening and sorting many posts, including irrelevant or duplicate ones (`01`, `04`, `06`, `07`, `08`); `03` struggles to find the right vacancy and `05` says public vacancies are still missed.
2. Why does sorting take effort? Opportunities appear across websites, company and university pages, and social channels (`01`, `03`, `04`, `05`, `06`), while some of those channels mix relevant posts with unrelated or repeated ones (`07`, `08`).

Root cause, one sentence: In the reported searches, dispersed sources and mixed or duplicate postings make students check opportunities manually to identify relevant ones (`01`, `04`, `06`, `07`, `08`).

Which of the answers above is a guess rather than a quote? The grouping of these experiences into one cause is our interpretation; the cited participants describe the separate steps. We cannot establish why the sources are fragmented or how many suitable opportunities actually exist.

## Pattern 2: Applicants spend extra effort establishing whether a posting actually fits them because eligibility or basic job terms are unclear or inconsistent.

Interviews behind it: `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09`.

1. Why does deciding whether to apply take extra effort? `01` opens posts to check eligibility and skipped positions when requirements or schedule were unclear; `08` messaged people to learn salary and hours.
2. Why can the initial posting fail to settle the decision? `06` found a “junior” requirement of three years' experience, `07` was invited to contact a “junior” posting but was then told experience was needed, and `08` found basic terms missing.
3. Interview 09 describes a posting with six office days, three languages, more than five years of experience, and leadership skills for 1500 GEL. This adds an example of requirements that may be inconsistent with the pay offered.

Root cause, one sentence: In these cases, the information needed to judge fit was absent, hard to locate, or inconsistent with the posting's apparent level or pay offered (`01`, `06`, `07`, `08`, `09`).

Which of the answers above is a guess rather than a quote? “Absent, hard to locate, or inconsistent” combines different reported cases; the interviews do not show why employers wrote the posts that way or whether a different applicant would have been accepted.

## Pattern 3: Some students discover job-related opportunities too late or miss them despite continuing to search.

Interviews behind it: `01`, `03`, `04`, `06`, `07`, `08`.

1. Why were opportunities missed? `01` found a vacancy after its deadline, `04` missed a TBC course, `06` learned of a legal clinic or internship too late, and `08` saw an internship after its deadline; `03` reports missing events while `07` gives an estimate without an example.
2. Why was the information not seen in time? `01` says searches were irregular, and `08` also reports irregular checking; `03`, `04` and `06` searched frequently yet still reported misses, so irregular checking cannot explain every case.

Root cause, one sentence: Timely discovery failed in several reported searches, but the interviews identify irregular checking as a contributor only for some participants and do not establish one shared deeper cause (`01`, `03`, `04`, `06`, `08`).

Which of the answers above is a guess rather than a quote? A common explanation such as poor notifications or late publication would be a guess: the interviews do not show when or where each missed opportunity was first announced. `03` explicitly says they do not forget deadlines, so missed events must not be treated as forgotten application deadlines.

## Pattern 4: Students assess opportunities against their studies and timetable, and schedule conflict can delay or prevent applications.

Interviews behind it: `01`, `02`, `04`, `06`, `08`.

1. Why is timetable fit part of the decision? `02` calls compatibility with university the most frustrating part, while `04`, `06` and `08` check required hours against study time; `01` checks whether a position meets their studies and schedule.
2. Why can this affect applying? `02` says they have sent no applications while focusing on studies, and `01` describes liked positions they did not apply to when requirements or schedule were uncertain; the others state selection criteria, not a documented abandoned application.

Root cause, one sentence: A student's class timetable and career aims constrain which opportunities appear feasible, and uncertainty about fit can delay or stop an application in the cases reported by `01` and `02`.

Which of the answers above is a guess rather than a quote? It would be a guess to say every participant rejected jobs because of schedule conflict, or that employers refuse flexible hours; neither claim is established by these interviews. `02`'s original message was not available for independent quote checking, although the team later confirmed consent for its use.

## Pattern 5: For some students, finding work also means tracking courses and career events they believe can lead to employment.

Interviews behind it: `03`, `04`, `06`.

1. Why do these participants consider opportunities beyond vacancies? `03` searches for courses and hackathons alongside jobs because they connect them to future employment; `04` describes company courses and `06` describes legal clinics and training as routes to work.
2. Why do they expect a connection to employment? `04` says companies may hire people after their courses, and `06` says legal clinics or training can lead to employment with the same organizations; these are participant beliefs, not independently measured hiring outcomes.

Root cause, one sentence: These participants understand entry into their fields as involving career-building activities as well as job applications (`03`, `04`, `06`).

Which of the answers above is a guess rather than a quote? The interviews do not verify actual hiring rates after courses or clinics, and `06` does not say they currently search for such programs. This pattern describes the scope of participants' search and a missed-course/clinic experience (`04`, `06`), not proof that a particular feature is needed.
