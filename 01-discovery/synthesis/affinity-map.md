# Affinity Map

Eight distinct interview logs were reviewed: `01`–`08`. `01` is the Saba–Anna interview; its supplied transcript is the same interview, not a ninth participant. Quotes from `03`–`06` were checked against `archilisinterviews.pdf`, and quotes from `07`–`08` against `anisinterviews.pdf`. The original messages behind `02` were not supplied, so its quotations are attributed to that log rather than independently verified against a transcript. A team member confirmed on 2026-10-11 that all participants knew their interviews would be used for the student project. The older `02` log still says “Consent: unfortunately no”; this discrepancy needs correction by its author. The log has not been changed.

For `01`, several lines labelled verbatim in the log differ from the supplied Saba–Anna transcript. The affected entries below use exact excerpts from that transcript instead; the original log has not been changed. Bracketed numbers identify interview logs, not numbers of mentions. Context and feature suggestions are kept in **Not yet a pattern** and do not count as evidence for a problem pattern.

## Pattern 1

Pattern: Searching across scattered sources and sorting through irrelevant posts makes relevant opportunities hard to find.

Interviews behind it (7): `01`, `03`, `04`, `05`, `06`, `07`, `08`.

### Quotes (verbatim, each tagged with its log number)

* `01`: “The most frustrating part was having to search through many different platforms and vacancies.”
* `03`: “The biggest hurdle is finding the right vacancy and the right place. I sent out my CV.”
* `04`: “You have to sort through each posting out of so many, read them, and check across several different websites at the same time.”
* `05`: “Searching is difficult; even though vacancies are public, I always end up missing a large number of them.”
* `06`: “It is exhausting having to sift through so many irrelevant job postings across different websites.”
* `07`: “Filtering was hard. On Facebook, many unrelated jobs showed up, and I had to spend most of my time sorting through them.”
* `08`: “Too many posts, and half of them are the same job posted again by different people.”

`08` specifically describes duplicated posts; other participants describe different forms of search effort. `02` describes schedule fit instead of this difficulty.

## Pattern 2

Pattern: Applicants spend extra effort establishing whether a posting actually fits them because eligibility or basic job terms are unclear or inconsistent.

Interviews behind it (4): `01`, `06`, `07`, `08`.

### Quotes (verbatim, each tagged with its log number)

* `01`: “I have to open many job posts to find out whether I'm actually eligible.”
* `01`: “there were also positions that I liked but didn't apply for because I wasn't sure whether they meet my requirements and my schedule” (supplied transcript; the log stops the quote before “and my schedule”).
* `06`: “You have to read through huge lists of requirements just to realize they want someone with 3 years of experience for a "junior" role, which makes it very hard to find the right entry-level spot.”
* `06`: “I didn't send my CV anywhere this time, I couldn't find a matching vacancy.”
* `07`: “The posts said junior level, so I contacted them”
* `07`: “They told me to contact them, and then said they needed experience.”
* `08`: “I had to message people just to ask basic things like the salary and the hours.”

The underlying details differ: `01`, `06` and `07` discuss eligibility or experience; `08` had to ask for salary and hours. The quotes do not show that every apparently unsuitable posting was actually suitable.

## Pattern 3

Pattern: Some students discover job-related opportunities too late or miss them despite continuing to search.

Interviews behind it (6): `01`, `03`, `04`, `06`, `07`, `08`.

### Quotes (verbatim, each tagged with its log number)

* `01`: “Sometimes I found an interesting vacancy after the deadline and had already passed, especially when I was not checking the platforms regularly.” (wording from the supplied transcript; the log paraphrases this line).
* `01`: “I don't have a fixed schedule, so I usually check when I have time or when I'm especially looking for an opportunity.” (supplied transcript; the log uses “specifically” instead of “especially”).
* `03`: “I never forget deadlines, but I have often missed important events. The last time it happened was a month or two ago.”
* `04`: “Several times. For example, I missed a TBC course (a course that helps you get employed by them if you pass).”
* `06`: “A few times. For example, I recently missed the deadline for a very good legal clinic/internship program at a government organization because I just didn't see the information in time.”
* `07`: “Maybe 2-3 times.” (answer to the missed-opportunity/deadline question; no particular example given)
* `08`: “Once I saw a really good internship, but the deadline was the day before.”

The opportunities differ: vacancies, courses, a clinic or internship, and events. `03` expressly says they do **not** forget deadlines; their issue is missing events. `02` did not recall an answer to this question.

## Pattern 4

Pattern: Students assess opportunities against their studies and timetable, and schedule conflict can delay or prevent applications.

Interviews behind it (5): `01`, `02`, `04`, `06`, `08`. `05` describes checking skill fit, but not an obstacle, so it is context rather than evidence of schedule conflict.

### Quotes (verbatim, each tagged with its log number)

* `01`: “I prefer to apply only when the position seems relevant to my skills, studies and experience.” (exact excerpt from the supplied transcript)
* `02`: “I have not sent the application to anywhere yet (Since I am trying to focus on my studies)”
* `02`: “The compatibility. Studying at the university and looking for the job which will align with the timetable is the most frustrating because those two go well together rarely.”
* `02`: “the job / internship must align with my studies, i.e. it must help me to develop my personal career.”
* `02`: “I am not sending any applications yet.” (also quoted under “Not answered” in log `02`)
* `04`: “Salary, and how much time it requires since I am also studying at the university.” (`archilisinterviews.pdf`, p. 2, Q6)
* `06`: “The working hours have to fit around my university schedule” (`archilisinterviews.pdf`, p. 4, Q6; exact excerpt)
* `08`: “whether the schedule works with my classes.” (`anisinterviews.pdf`, second interview, Q6; exact excerpt)

For `04`, `06` and `08`, the quotes establish selection criteria; they do not by themselves prove that an application was abandoned. `02` directly reports delay. `01` reports choosing only relevant positions.

## Not yet a pattern

These are exact participant statements retained from the logs or original sources. They give context, describe a workaround, or suggest a feature, but do not independently establish another recurring problem.

* `01`: “I will find it useful to have one platform where relevant opportunities are collected in one place” (supplied transcript; feature suggestion, not evidence that this specific solution works).
* `02`: “I am still actively searching for new opportunities.” (search status)
* `02`: “especially in the Germany since I have been studying the German language since I was in the school” (distinct interest in opportunities abroad)
* `02`: “Searching the specific platforms with AI makes it easier to find the job.” (reported workaround)
* `03`: “Various websites, and offers provided by the university.” (sources used)
* `03`: “I am also interested in hackathons and courses because they are closely related to my future employment, so I always look for them along with jobs!” (adjacent opportunities sought)
* `04`: “jobs.ge, LinkedIn, and also on the pages of various companies like TBC, BOG, EPAM.” (sources used)
* `04`: “Also, it would be great to have something that provides hackathons, internships, and courses, because, for example, after completing courses from EPAM, TBC, or Quantori, they employ you themselves.” (feature suggestion and perceived career path)
* `05`: “From KIU's (Kutaisi International University) information channels, companies' official vacancy websites, and friends.” (sources used)
* `05`: “If they require the skills that I have developed, then I realize this vacancy is for me.” (selection criterion)
* `05`: “Yes, jobs.ge, ChatGPT, LinkedIn, etc.” (tools used)
* `08`: “I messaged two places and sent my CV to one.” (outcome of one search, without a stated problem in this sentence)

The examples of courses, hackathons and legal training show that some participants look beyond job advertisements. They do not yet show that a separate product feature is needed. The original hypothesis about a general shortage of jobs and unfair compensation is not established by the quotes above; this map records the interview evidence without deciding the hypothesis verdict.
